Privacy — Budget Tracker

Last updated: 22 September 2026
This is a personal expense-tracking app built and used by a single person (its owner). It is not a commercial product, does not accept public sign-ups, and this page is a plain, factual description of what the app does with data — not a formal legal document for a public service.

What the app stores

Everything you log in the app — transaction amounts, merchant names, categories, budgets, vehicle and fuel records, to-do items — is stored in a private Postgres database the owner controls. Access to that database is protected by Row-Level Security; the app itself is protected by a private access token known only to its owner.

How bank alerts are captured

Transactions are detected from two sources, both under the owner's own control:

  • SMS forwarding — a phone automation app forwards the owner's own bank SMS messages to the app.
  • Gmail (read-only) — the app connects to one Gmail inbox via Google's OAuth API, with read-only access. It only reads emails from specific bank sender addresses (e.g. a bank's official alert address) to detect transaction alerts. It does not read, store, or process any other email in the inbox.

The relevant parts of a matched alert (amount, merchant name, a masked account number fragment, and the bank's own transaction reference) are stored in the app's private database so the transaction can be shown, categorised, and totalled. This data is used only to run the app for its owner. It is never sold, shared with third parties, or used for advertising.

Third-party processing

Merchant text (e.g. "SWIGGY", "coffee shop") is occasionally sent to an AI provider — Google's Gemini API or Azure OpenAI — solely to guess a spending category (e.g. "Food & Dining"). No account numbers or full alert text are sent for this; only the merchant name and amount. This is the only third-party data processing the app performs.

What this app does not do

  • No ads, no ad tracking, no analytics beyond what's needed for the app itself to work.
  • No data is sold or shared with anyone besides the processing described above.
  • No public sign-up — this app has exactly one user, its owner.
